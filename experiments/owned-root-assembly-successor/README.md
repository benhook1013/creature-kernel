## Status and authority

This is the settled candidate plan for one new, bounded, standard-neutral
experiment. It is the successor direction authorized after the seven-ring
candidate in PR #125 reached its three-round stop rule. It does not accept a
production schema, architecture, topology, supported morphology promise, or

The previous candidate is evidence, not a foundation to transplant. Its

## Protocol

This is an unregistered exploratory experiment. No comparative, accounting,

## Question

Can a single welded surface assembled from explicit, interacting anatomical
domains produce a credible standard-neutral ribcage-to-pelvis root complex
with a readable neck, shoulder/axilla junctions, and hip/thigh roots, without
any layer silently bypassing or rewriting another?

This is a representation-direction question. It is not a claim that the
result is a complete humanoid, an animation-ready body, a runtime skeleton,
or a production geometry backend.

## Hypothesis

Giving thorax, neck root, abdomen/lumbar bridge, pelvis, bilateral
shoulder/axilla roots, and bilateral hip roots explicit surface ownership,
local charts, and shared welded junctions will make the causal path from
authored anatomy to evaluated skin inspectable and will produce clearer root
anatomy than the failed single axial ring grammar.

The hypothesis fails if the candidate needs unowned geometry, duplicated or
hidden skin, render-only corrections, profile-specific geometry, a solver, or
more than the allowed shared correction rounds to make the neutral result
credible.

## Decision impact

A credible neutral result, after all technical gates, permits an unchanged
exact-five profile run and a later human checkpoint about whether this
representation is worth extending. It does not approve production use.

An uncured neutral failure rejects this candidate. It does not reject
programmatic or hybrid surfaces globally. A materially different
representation then requires a new recorded runway and direction; this
experiment does not silently fall back to another approach.

## Ephemeral experiment interfaces

The following are conceptual boundaries for this experiment only. They are not
serialized production schemas, canonical identifiers, or additions to
`spec/`:

- **Prepared intent**: admitted source addresses and roles, frames, landmarks,
  section measurements, module state, and provenance.
- **Domain plan**: one owner for each domain interior and its local chart,
  including declared boundary participation.
- **Junction plan**: the shared boundary between incident domains, its ordered
  interface curve, port relationship, and continuity obligations.
- **Causal binding**: source address/role to derived parameter, control,
  subdivision stencil, and evaluated-surface lineage.
- **Surface artifact**: one welded control/evaluated surface with ephemeral
  domain, junction, port, chart, contributor, and diagnostic metadata.

The construction layer owns base faces, domain interiors, and junction
construction. The semantic layer carries causality from authored addresses and
roles through derived parameters and subdivision stencils. The evaluated
surface carries contributor and chart lineage, which may name multiple
domains. These layers may reference one another through explicit bindings but
may not silently replace one another.

Every base face and control has exactly one construction owner: a domain
interior or a declared junction. Every shared junction boundary is represented
once in the welded surface and records all incident domains. Every evaluated
surface element has valid contributor/chart lineage. There is no unowned
output. A categorical colour or legend may expose lineage in a diagnostic,
but it is never evidence of causality by itself.

## Fixed scope and hard caps

The standard-neutral root surface contains five open anatomy ports: neck,
left arm, right arm, left thigh, and right thigh. This candidate uses exactly
eight named, nonempty domain interiors while retaining the hard `<=8` cap:

- thorax;
- neck root;
- abdomen/lumbar bridge;
- pelvis;
- left shoulder/axilla root;
- right shoulder/axilla root;
- left hip root; and
- right hip root.

The hard experiment caps are fixed before the design pass:

- at most 8 explicit domains;
- exactly 5 declared open ports for the neutral root surface;
- at most 128 base controls and 120 base quads;
- exactly 2 declared subdivision levels;
- at most 2 shared correction rounds after the initial build.

These are caps, not a chosen topology. The experiment must not claim an exact
control count, boundary cardinality, patch degree, extraordinary-vertex
placement, or coordinate layout until the bounded design pass below freezes
those details. All eight named domain interiors must be nonempty. If the
design cannot satisfy the caps and ownership rules, it stops before
coordinates are authored.

This pass freezes obligations and measurable tolerances before geometry

units and rationale before the build; human visual criteria remain
authoritative for anatomical readability.

## Topology and junction requirements

The candidate is one connected, orientable, welded surface with the five
declared open ports. It must have no duplicate overlapping skin pieces,
unowned faces, seam-only contacts, accidental holes, or topology selected by a
profile. Every boundary and junction is explicit in the design record.

The junction network must make these relationships inspectable:

- a neck-root junction that exposes a neck stem rather than a mound in the
  thorax;
- shoulder/axilla junctions whose shoulders depart below the neck and leave a
  readable axillary hollow around each arm port;
- an abdomen/lumbar bridge between thorax and pelvis rather than one universal
  torso ring progression; and
- hip-root junctions where each pelvis wraps over and seats the thigh root,
  with the thighs descending from beneath the pelvis.

Shared boundaries use the same control curve, not two surfaces made to
coincide. The candidate must report positional continuity and the frozen
normal/fold measures. It must not claim mathematical C1 or G1 continuity at
extraordinary vertices or open ports without a separate proof.

## Causality and ownership requirements

Before rendering, a preflight must prove:

1. Every admitted geometry-driving source address, role, landmark, frame,
   section measure, module-state input, and shared scalar has a downstream
   causal consumer or is explicitly classified as non-geometric.
2. Every domain interior, junction, port, base face, and control has exactly
   one construction owner.
3. Every evaluated element carries contributor/chart lineage, including
   multi-domain junction contributions where applicable.
4. Each must-affect definition names a baseline, perturbation, in-target
   evaluated-vertex set, scale `S`, frozen minimum movement relative to `S`,
   off-target set, and frozen locality tolerance.
5. A fresh baseline-versus-perturbed build must move actual in-target evaluated
   vertex coordinates by at least that scale-relative minimum, and the final
   mesh bytes must differ by serializing those changed coordinates.
6. Off-target evaluated vertex coordinates remain within their frozen locality
   tolerance.
7. Metadata, provenance, dependency, manifest, or ownership-label movement
   without the required evaluated-coordinate and final-mesh movement fails.
8. Topology, IDs, formulas, and ownership remain unchanged under admitted
   perturbations.
9. The renderer consumes the evaluated surface and its lineage; it cannot
   add geometry, alter vertices, or use a second hidden skin.

Provenance is not proof of effect. A listed dependency with no geometric
effect fails the must-affect gate. Conversely, a contributor label without a
causal binding is not accepted as an explanation.

## Preflight and build order

The eventual implementation must follow this order and stop at the first
failed gate:

1. Verify worktree, source paths, source hashes, pinned launcher, and the
   standard-neutral-only scope.
2. Create and SHA-256-bind the required design contract; no later build may
   choose an omitted topology, ownership, cap, tolerance, or evidence rule.
3. Admit and validate the prepared intent, including provenance and the
   required `neck_upper` and `upper_abdomen` source controls.
4. Build the domain, junction, port, and chart ownership plan; prove complete
   coverage and a single shared boundary representation.
5. Build the causal binding plan from source roles through derived parameters,
   controls, subdivision support, and evaluated lineage.
6. Run symbolic topology and ownership preflight before admitting anatomy
   coordinates; reject profile-selected layout, duplicated boundaries, or
   unowned output.
7. Derive one welded control surface from the admitted intent using shared
   rules. No render correction, solver, optimizer, remesher, or post-generation
   edit is permitted.
8. Evaluate exactly the frozen subdivision support for two levels, retaining
   the base and intermediate diagnostics.
9. Run structural, geometric, causality, locality, and determinism gates;
   produce the direct-skin and same-surface ownership evidence.
10. Run the fixed neutral visual appraisal. Only a credible neutral result
    unlocks the exact-five gate.

## Structural and geometric gates

The following gates apply to the actual welded evaluated surface and its base
construction, not to a synthetic diagnostic object:

- all values are finite and the surface is connected, orientable, and a valid
  welded quad surface with exactly the five declared ports;
- control/quadrilateral counts remain within the fixed caps and all topology,
  junction, port, and extraordinary rules match the frozen design;
- no face, edge, boundary, or control is unowned or duplicated;
- shared junctions satisfy the frozen positional continuity and normal/fold
  bounds;
- no degenerate edges/faces, invalid winding, accidental holes, or
  non-adjacent self-intersections exist at either evaluated level;
- the five ports preserve their frozen orientation and remain usable after
  subdivision;
- neck, both axillae, groin, and both medial-thigh clearances remain positive
  under the candidate-specific geometric measures;
- candidate-specific measures report neck exposure, shoulder descent,
  axillary concavity, pelvic wrap, and downward thigh-root orientation
  independently; and
- all must-affect/locality, forbidden-input, profile-neutrality, and
  deterministic-artifact checks pass.

Clearance and continuity thresholds are not invented in this plan. The design
pass must freeze them before coordinates and record each independently; one
aggregate score is forbidden. Human vision decides whether positive geometry
actually reads as anatomy.

## Visual evidence and checkpoint

Render the direct skin from the same evaluated surface for fixed front, side,
and three-quarter views. The ownership/contributor diagnostic must reuse those
same evaluated positions and topology; it may add a transparent legend or
lineage overlay but may not render a hidden structural substitute.

The neutral internal visual gate looks for a clear neck stem, shoulders
departing below it, arm ports below that departure, visible axillary hollows,
pelvis wrapping over the hip roots, and thighs descending from beneath the
pelvis. It also records whether the thorax, abdomen/lumbar bridge, and pelvis
read as deliberate connected regions rather than primitive blobs or a single
ring-shaped mass. This is scoped evidence, not an aggregate aesthetic score.

No gallery or human-facing checkpoint is created before the neutral result is
credible and the required technical evidence exists. A later exact-five
gallery, if unlocked, must show direct final skin and the same-surface lineage
diagnostic; an x-ray-like picture must not imply an underlying structure that
was not rendered.

## Deterministic artifact policy

The implementation must use the repository's pinned experiment launcher and a
fresh temporary output directory for every run. It must record the exact
interpreter/environment identity, source hashes, design-contract identity,
and artifact hashes. Generated meshes, captures, caches, and renders remain
outside Git unless a storage decision is accepted.

Two fresh launcher processes with different `PYTHONHASHSEED` values must
produce byte-identical prepared input, base and evaluated mesh artifacts,
lineage/dependency and metrics manifests, and metadata-free visual evidence.
Paths, timestamps, host-specific display data, and temporary-directory names
must not affect identity. A failed or partial publication must not be mistaken
for evidence.

The eventual README revision that activates execution must provide the exact
copy-paste reproduction command and list all raw outputs. This plan remains
non-executable until that command and the freeze-before-build record exist.

## Correction stop

The initial implementation is followed by at most two shared correction
rounds. A round is one coherent, review-triggered change to already-frozen
shared construction formulas or tunables, applied consistently to both sides
and all relevant domains, followed by full regeneration and inspection.

A correction may not change the patch layout, port cardinality, topology,
ownership model, source admission, subdivision rule, gates, or scope. Such a
change is a new candidate and requires a new runway; it cannot be hidden as a
third correction. Render-only offsets, profile branches, duplicated skin,
solver behaviour, and threshold relaxation are immediate stop conditions.

If the neck, shoulder/axilla, and hip/thigh-root visual criteria are not
credible after the initial build plus two shared corrections, mark this
candidate rejected/inconclusive in its eventual evidence record, archive it,
and stop. Do not run exact-five, add a tail, build distal anatomy, or continue
parameter tuning.

## Exact-five gate

Exact-five is a later unchanged-rule gate, not part of the initial neutral
implementation. It may run only when:

1. the neutral design and all structural, geometric, causality, locality, and
   deterministic gates pass;
2. main-thread vision finds the required neck, shoulder/axilla, pelvic-wrap,
   and thigh-root cues credible; and
3. the exact five existing profile identities are selected only as admitted
   data, in this order, with the standard reference first:
   `standard_neutral_reference`,
   `compact_broad_short_limb_large_head`,
   `tall_narrow_long_legged`, `slender_long_limb`, and
   `stocky_broad_chested`.

The five profiles use the same domain plan, topology, junction rules,
causality rules, subdivision support, gates, and renderer. No profile-specific
geometry or topology is permitted. A tail-present/tail-absent case is not
part of this gate; optional-module absence requires a separately derived,
explicitly authorized input and must not create phantom identities.

Reuse is conditional, not inherited from PR #125. The main thread audits each
candidate against representation-independent inputs, consumers, assumptions,

| Prepared-input admission and provenance helpers | Adapt or reimplement | Retain only after rejecting unknown/empty provenance and forbidden final-geometry inputs; test the successor's admitted fields directly. |
| Seven-ring topology, axial projection, shoulder/seat/envelope/saddle formulas, pelvic macro, control IDs, and thresholds | Archive/reimplement | They encode the failed candidate's ownership and layout; they are not a successor foundation. |

An item may be retained only when it has no seven-ring/profile assumptions,
has an isolated interface, has independent tests for the successor use, and
has lower maintenance risk than a small reimplementation. The ledger itself
does not authorize promotion into production code or canonical architecture.
