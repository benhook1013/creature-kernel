# Connected-leg assembly example brief

Status: Short exploratory example brief; not a morphology contract, protocol,
execution input, or production promise.

Provenance: Ben's approval relayed through the Overseer on 2026-09-06.

Ben's direct feedback on the initial Baseline002 rest images on 2026-09-06
was that they read too female for his preferred default. Human and
anthropomorphic examples should use a male/masculine-looking default. He
singled out the narrow waist with full hips and thighs, and the lateral chest
bulge; the chest bulge is an anatomical form defect, not merely gender
styling. This does not exclude female examples, assert a biologically
universal proportion rule, imply an adult context, or make a species claim.

Ben approved two loose appearance references for the common exploratory
pipeline:

- an ordinary human biped;
- a loose wolf-like humanoid biped with a simplified muzzle, upright ears,
  tail, and a coarse digitigrade/paw-like lower limb.

The species reference is for loose appearance only. It is not detailed anatomy,
taxonomy, fur, a production promise, or motivation for a separate branch.

Both references use the common pipeline. A temporary shared plantigrade stage
may be used for connection and staging, but it must be labelled temporary and
must not redefine the anthropomorphic target. No per-case mesh fixes or
species-name geometry branches are permitted.

This updates the example appraisal reference only, not current frozen inputs or
a formal product contract. The existing leg-motion/source-response baseline
and its no-retune, no-regeneration, no-protocol-change instruction remain
historical routing for that baseline. The subsequently authorized integrated
refinement is now active through explicit captured inputs, including the
masculine overall proportion balance and chest/shoulder-attachment defect;
this does not expand authority or change the Ben checkpoint.
